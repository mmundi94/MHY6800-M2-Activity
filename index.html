Nomli brief fictional

Nomli, its people, its partners, and its numbers are fictional and were written for this practice.

  • About 40 people. Nomli's app lets parents pay school fees by phone.
  • Public releases go out once a month. A test version can reach pilot users within a week.
  • The CTO is starting three projects and asks: "Should we run them all the same way?"

Goal: let parents split a term's fees into smaller payments.

  • Nobody knows yet how many payments parents want to split a term into, or which options they'll trust.
  • Fee Planner succeeds only if parents actually set up payment plans and keep them.
  • Bursars at five pilot schools can meet the team every two weeks.
  • There's no external deadline. A test version can reach pilot parents within a week, and changing it costs little.

Goal: send each school's daily fee payments to Nomli's partner bank in the bank's file format.

  • The bank contract sets out the file in full: 9 fields, their order, and the allowed values. Any change needs written agreement from both sides.
  • The bank contract says the file must be live by the last day of term, and the bank won't accept a partial file.
  • The bank's IT team can join one call a month and one two-week test window.
  • After the test window, any change to the file must be retested by the bank, which takes about six weeks.
  • Parents won't see anything new. Feedback comes from the bank, not from parents.

Goal: send parents a receipt after every payment.

  • Nomli's contract with schools says receipts must be live in 10 weeks.
  • Five fields are agreed with schools: amount, date, school, student number, and payment reference.
  • Still open: how a receipt should look on a basic phone, and whether parents want it by text message or in the app.
  • Two pilot schools can review drafts every two weeks.
  • Once receipts go live at all schools, any change means retraining every school's bursar.
